\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\subsection{The cohort is bounded, and nothing here is a prevalence estimate}

The roster of 47 is a protocol-defined discovery cohort re-derived around four inherited
studies. It is not a sample of the coding-agent literature, and the direction of what it
misses is unknown: we cannot say whether unrostered work binds its artifacts better or
worse, so the cohort's counts are neither a lower nor an upper bound on any population
quantity. Every count is reported with its denominator named and none is converted into a
rate over the literature.

Selection enters at more than one point, and moving artifact availability out of the
eligibility rule closes only one of them. By not conditioning cohort eligibility on artifact
availability, we avoid enriching the cohort for readily auditable studies by construction;
availability is instead measured for every rostered record. What remains open is the
inherited selection of the four deep-audit studies (\S\ref{sec:method:provenance}), the 22
of 47 rostered papers that expose no locator, a discovery frame seeded from those same four
studies, and seven records adjudicated into scope after the roster was frozen and held out of
it. The tier-two sample was drawn, but compatibility depth was not executed.

Twenty decoys drawn from the first axis's \texttt{OUT\_OF\_SCOPE} bucket were judged blind by the second
axis, and two were called in scope. This is an observed directional disagreement of 2/20
without a gold label, not a false-negative rate. It does not support extrapolation to the
463 first-axis \texttt{OUT\_OF\_SCOPE} records or to the literature.

After sibling resolution, the second axis also judged 19 first-axis in-scope records in a
non-blind coverage batch. That batch extended coverage but is not independent axis-quality
evidence and is excluded from the 45/58 blinded-agreement calculation.

A precision layer set 2{,}390 records aside in the two judgeable buckets before any axis
judged them. A seeded sample of 100 of those records was adjudicated: four were in scope and
none of the four carries a peer-reviewed record. That sample estimates the rate at which our
operational procedure would call a record in the residual pool additionally eligible; it does
not estimate the completeness or representativeness of the cohort, and 0 of 100 does not mean
nothing was missed. The interval is over records rather than unique papers, so its upper
bound is a record-level figure and is not comparable to a count of studies.

The current corrected corpus records contain 237 unresolved rows and 214 unresolved records
after title deduplication. These are unresolved records, not excluded studies, and they do not
support a claim about how many eligible studies remain outside the frozen roster.

\subsection{Absence of evidence is separated from evidence of absence throughout}

Three distinctions carry weight and each is preserved in the data rather than in prose.

A paywall is not an absent artifact. Of the 22 studies whose papers expose no locator, 10 are
determinate -- the full text was read and names no artifact host -- and 12 are undetermined,
9 because no open full text was located and 3 because the located text was not retrievable.

\textsc{not found} is not \textsc{absent}. The corpus judgement reads the deposit's listing
and the prose beside it. A binding stated in a paper appendix, a release note, or a branch
that was not read would not be seen, and the reported figure is therefore an upper bound on
absence.

An unresolved preprint is not an unpublished study. One confirmed study is in the pool only
as its arXiv record because its proceedings venue is under-indexed by every channel that
answered.

\subsection{The corpus claim is read, because the pattern screen was measured and failed}

An automated screen was built first and is not what the corpus claim rests on. Measured
against blind readings of the same evidence it disagreed on 8 of 25 studies, and the
disagreements were not noise. It attributed to one paper a binding sentence belonging to a
copy of a third-party agent vendored inside that paper's repository. It missed released
per-run evidence exported as CSV, because its file-name patterns did not cover that
extension. It recorded run outputs for a release archive on the strength of the substring
\texttt{eval} in its name. It reported one study as publishing no run outputs when that
study's traces, predictions and harness logs are published as downloads linked from the
repository rather than committed inside it. Every one of these is a defect of pattern
matching standing in for reading, and tightening the patterns further would only move the
error rate without measuring it, since the studies available to tune on are the same ones the
claim is about.

The 25 studies were therefore judged by reading a blind evidence dump per study, with the
screen's verdicts withheld while judging and recorded beside each judgement afterwards. The
screen remains in the artifact as the evidence-gathering step -- it retrieves listings,
release assets and prose -- and its verdicts are reported only as the comparison above.

The judgement is by one rater and is not blinded to the study's identity, which the deposit
itself reveals. Its inputs, its per-study rationale and the disagreeing screen verdicts are
released together so a disputed call can be checked against the same evidence.

Whose artifact a locator names is itself a judgement. Papers cite their own artifact in their
reference list, footnote it without a sentence, and name third-party tools in prose the same
way, so no positional rule separates them: harvesting full text without this distinction gave
one paper the repositories of two agents it merely built on. Each locator therefore carries a
recorded provenance -- the repository is named after the paper, an offering sentence sits
beside it, an artifact word introduces it, or it is only mentioned in passing -- and every
locator was screened whatever its provenance, with ownership settled at judgement with the
deposit's contents in hand. Five of the 25 were judged not to be the paper's own.

\subsection{Tier-two sample drawn; compatibility analysis not executed}

The recorded design would draw $\min(4, |\text{class}|)$ studies per tier-one availability
class under seed 20260809. The realized draw contains 18 non-census studies; adding the four
inherited census-stratum studies yields a tier-two set of 22. No per-class compatibility analysis
was run. The paper therefore reports no per-class result, pooled compatibility rate, transfer
verdict, or study-level attemptability judgement. The completed corpus work is the extraction
design and availability/disposition screen, including the 22 rostered papers that expose no
locator.

\subsection{Construct: what the audit measures}

The audit measures whether published evidence supports re-deriving a published comparison.
It does not measure whether the comparison is correct. We re-ran no agent and re-evaluated
no patch, and tier-two attemptability was not judged. No actual deposit was established in
advance as a known-positive control under the v2 rule. The zero identified decisions therefore
cannot calibrate the audit's sensitivity to a real positive.

The item states also depend on a preregistered required-item vector per comparison, frozen
before retrieval. A different but defensible derivation of which items a comparison requires
would move the item counts. The v2 verdicts follow the recorded outcome-specific rule and the
enumerable public candidates; they do not prove that every defensible alternative criterion
would produce the same states.

\subsection{The target specification was re-derived blind, and our own record had to be
repaired first}

Every verdict here is conditional on one target specification: what each decision's reported
outcome is, which components its recovery requires, and which evidence chains would suffice.
Freezing that specification before adjudication, as \S\ref{sec:results:compatibility} records,
establishes that it was not edited to fit a verdict. It does not establish that it is right. A
mis-specified outcome makes the audit search for the wrong evidence, find none, and report
non-exposure; nothing downstream of the specification can detect that.

We therefore re-derived it. Two model axes with no access to this project's records were each
given one decision's identifier, its source document, an empty five-coordinate schema, and the
full text of the source, and asked to derive the outcome, the benchmark frame, the condition and
repetition structure, the applicable and inapplicable coordinates, and two independent
properties of the released evidence: whether it identifies the exact reported point estimate,
and whether it could contradict it. Answers were forced into integers and closed vocabularies
so the comparison is mechanical, every quotation was verified against the source, and
disagreement survived as unresolved rather than being reconciled. Each axis first decides
whether the material it was given picks out one quantity at all, and an axis that says it does
not writes no manifest.

The first pass failed for a reason that is ours, not the sources'. Six decisions cited the
character-identical location \emph{Section 4.2 and Table 3}, and three further groups collided
once overlapping location strings were counted, so an axis was being asked about a passage
rather than a decision -- and answered accordingly, returning the same denominator and numerator
for a resolution comparison and for a cost comparison that cite one table.

The repair keeps the audit out of the pointer. Each decision inherits the numeric literals its
source prints for it, taken mechanically from our register's value column: a set of printed
figures locates a cell without stating what the cell measures, what it is over, how it
aggregates, or what would recover it. Every literal is required to occur in its source and every
set to be unique among the twenty. Under that pointer all twenty decisions are individuated.

Fourteen of the twenty then join. On the property this paper's result rests on -- whether the
released evidence identifies the exact reported point estimate -- \textbf{twelve of the fourteen
agree that it does not}, and two split, with the disagreeing axis in each case being the one
that answered yes. That is blind corroboration of the central finding rather than a restatement
of it: neither axis could see our records, our verdicts, or each other. The second property is
not settled the same way. Whether released evidence could \emph{contradict} a printed number is
a weaker and more permissive question, and the axes agree on it in seven of fourteen, with one
axis answering yes throughout; we therefore report non-identification as corroborated and
contestability as open. Across the fourteen the evidence is 661 quotations matched exactly,
five matched only across an extraction splice, 174 recorded absences, and none unmatched.

The six that do not join are three different things, and none of them is unfinished work. \textbf{Three} were refused by both axes independently because the value cell
\emph{we} recorded does not name one compared quantity: \texttt{CMP-034} carries the
three-repetition row alongside the single-attempt pair, \texttt{CMP-024} carries two model rows
of one table against two separate claims, and \texttt{CMP-004} records an ablation range whose
lower bound is not an ablation value at all but a figure from a different table, for a different
system, on a different split from the one the decision is about. A fourth, \texttt{CMP-018},
recorded a leaderboard rank in the same cell as the value; that one is mechanically repairable
against the source's own column header, and once the rank is removed the axis that had refused
the decision binds it and it joins. So for three of twenty audited decisions our own record
carries the defect this paper reports in its sources -- a cell that does not identify one
compared quantity -- and repairing them is not available to us, because choosing which recorded
value a decision is \emph{about} would be authoring the target semantics the axes exist to
derive independently. \textbf{Two} were refused on quotation: one axis substituted a connective,
writing \emph{Furthermore} where its source reads \emph{In addition}, and in the other the axis
quoted table rows whose cells the extraction emits in a different order than the row.

The last, \texttt{CMP-041}, is the case that says most about the gate. Its cell records two
values because \emph{the source prints two}: the text gives 19.7\% at $k{=}1$ rising to 26.3\%
at $k{=}8$, while Figure~4 labels the same curve 19.1\% and 26.0\%. One axis bound the text pair
and the other the figure pair; neither is wrong, and no single target manifest exists. Our
register had already logged that conflict, and the axes reached it from the sources alone with
no access to the register -- so the gate is detecting real ambiguity in the sources, not only
our own bookkeeping. A further split had no defect behind it at all: our coordinate extractor
had taken the \emph{4} out of the model name \emph{GPT-4 Turbo}, and both axes then located it
in the source as a \$4 per-instance budget, a parameter of the run rather than a value of the
compared quantity. The extractor now refuses a digit hanging off a hyphen that follows a
letter, and that decision joins.

The quotation check is worth stating because an earlier version of this subsection reported the
wrong repair for it. Reading a two-column source in reading order splices foreign material into
the middle of a sentence -- a page number, a row from the table beside the paragraph -- so an
axis that quotes what a human reads as one sentence writes something the byte stream does not
contain contiguously. We first proposed to warn the axes to quote only uninterrupted stretches.
That would have taught the instrument to cite away from tables and page breaks, biasing which
evidence is offered in order to make our checker pass, and hiding a defect that is ours. The
check was changed instead: a quotation may cross bounded extraction debris, subject to limits on
how many interruptions, how wide, how short a run may resume on, and how much of the quotation
may be debris, with order never negotiable. Such matches are counted separately rather than
called exact -- five of 666 here -- and the two refusals above survive the relaxation, which is
what tells us it did not admit everything regardless.

Two axes filling one schema can be wrong together wherever the schema is wrong, and their
agreement cannot show it, because neither answer has a field for what the schema omitted. We
therefore ran a second, open-ended derivation on a sample fixed in advance: one joined
decision per study, drawn by a seeded script before any prompt was written, with the protocol
and the draw recorded first. Exactly one thing was removed. The axes were asked, in prose and
with no field list, what the decision reports, what a reproducer would have to obtain, and
whether the released evidence is enough to arrive at the printed number or to contradict it.
The coordinates were kept, because removing them changes which decision is being described.

The probe found three components the schema has no field for, so the schema-bound counts above
are conditional on a schema now known to be incomplete. Most of each open answer maps cleanly,
and the largest group that does not map neatly -- prompts, seeds, decoding settings, scaffold
versions -- is deliberately not counted among the three, since the release identity and the
sufficiency judgement between them already carry it.

The clearest is a contradiction that needs no released evidence at all, which is what
\texttt{contestable} is defined to exclude. For \texttt{CMP-011} the source reports
Stuck-in-Loop rates over a stated 500 instances, and at that denominator the attainable
percentages fall on a 0.2 grid. Four of the six printed cells are exact counts --
39.6\% is 198/500, 21.0\% is 105, 29.4\% is 147, 23.8\% is 119 -- and two are not reachable by
any integer count at all: 32.1\% is 160.5 tasks and 21.3\% is 106.5. The same row disagrees
with itself again in its printed differences, two of which are the difference of their printed
operands while the third is not: $32.1 - 21.3 = 10.8$, printed as $-10.7$. What this
contradicts is a reading -- that each cell counts one completed trajectory per instance over
500 -- and the source chooses between the readings that survive nowhere; a per-row denominator
below 500, or more than one trajectory per instance, would each accommodate the figures. The
point for this paper is that the contradiction is a property of the published table, available
with no artifact, and that neither schema-bound axis reported it because the schema gave it
nowhere to go.

The second is the evaluator's own conduct. \texttt{evaluator\_components} carries exactly four
keys and an answer with any other set is rejected, so what the judges were told, whether they
judged blind to which system produced a patch, and how often they disagreed cannot be recorded
at all. A procedure for resolving disagreement and a record of its rate are different things,
and \texttt{CMP-035}'s source supplies the first and not the second. The third and weakest is
the presentation transform: the step from counts to a printed figure, which this paper's own
prose names separately from aggregation while the schema offers one field for both.

None of the three would turn a \emph{not identified} into an \emph{identified}, which is the
direction this result runs; they bear on how a printed value relates to its counts and on the
evaluator's procedure. And four decisions is four. Nothing here estimates how many of the
remaining sixteen carry an uncaptured component, and the sample was drawn from the decisions
that join, which are not a random subset of the twenty. What the probe establishes is that
this is a schema an open reading can still add to -- it added the comparator once already, and
that component is now a field.

Three limits remain even so. Two model axes share training and prompting and can be wrong
together, so agreement is weak evidence, the open probe above bounds that rather than removing
it, and no human re-derivation was performed. Whether our register
recorded the right figures in the cells that did join is upstream of this check and stays
unexamined; the layer can only see a defect that makes a cell ambiguous, not one that makes it
wrong. And the layer's own verdicts moved as its contract was repaired -- an earlier contract,
with a single sufficiency field and no component structure for the evaluator, joined all twenty
-- so what is reported here is a count under one stated contract, and the contract, like the
specification it checks, is ours.

\subsection{Researcher degrees of freedom, disclosed}

The eligible-decision denominator moved during specification, from 43 to 37 to 39 to 20, as
the eligibility rule was corrected against the claim-type record. Anything computed on the
retired denominators is reported only as an audit of the retired rule. The frozen corpus and
required-item vectors were recorded before the outcomes they bear on were inspected. The
two-tier split, sampling seed, and realized draw are design and selection records; they produced
no tier-two compatibility outcomes.
