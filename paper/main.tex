\begin{abstract}
A published coding-agent comparison reports numbers; its artifact contains bytes. We audit
the join between them. This is a four-study, twenty-decision, protocolized audit: a codebook
fixed before any availability lookup was operationalized into a vector of 206 recorded
evidence requirements, and we retrieve 2.8\,GB of released artifacts. The released-byte
checks are deliberately bounded: exact task-manifest equality for SWE-agent, resolved/empty
arithmetic for a selected SWE-Gym Moatless subset, the Agentless generation archive, and
AutoCodeRover run-report and candidate arithmetic. We do not claim full recomputation
for every study. The 20 comparison decisions are nested within the four studies, and the 206
requirements are nested within the decisions; none of these counts is a sample size.

Under a criterion whose rule identity is recorded by digest -- can a reproducer determine
the release set, run-output set, evaluator and configuration, task universe and aggregation
rule behind a reported figure, without picking the candidate whose value matches it? -- the
outcome-specific v2 re-adjudication classifies all 20 decisions as \textsc{unidentified}.
None is \textsc{identified} or \textsc{compatibility not evaluated}, and all five condition
groups across the four studies are \textsc{not identified}. \textsc{Unidentified} means that
the public record exposes no candidate for at least one outcome-specific component, not that
the object never existed.
The planned tier-two, per-class compatibility analysis was not run, so the paper reports no
transfer-attemptability or per-class compatibility result.

The bounded checks surface what neither source nor artifact shows alone. AutoCodeRover's
source reports three repetitions of its base configuration, cites the standard $k=1$ estimator,
and annotates two of its three printed Lite figures accordingly -- 19.00\% (57) at @1 and
26.00\% (78) at @3 -- while ACR-sbfl at 22.00\% (66) carries no run-count annotation and no
stated run count anywhere. Its public run-output reports expose base-run triples of
54/57/57 under the new evaluation and 48/47/50 under the old, with corresponding ACR-sbfl
counts of 66 and 61. The 9--14 range comprises raw per-repetition contrasts; applying the
cited estimator to complete triples gives source-unbound differences of 10 and $38/3$.
Because the source supplies neither a public-bundle binding nor the complete selection/count
presentation transform, the arithmetic selects no effect and does not change the verdict.

The four studies were inherited rather than sampled, so we make no representativeness claim.
Around them we re-derive a bounded discovery cohort: the inherited frame is a property of a
search in which seven of eighteen channels returned an error before a result page, and
re-running discovery under a protocol frozen before outcome coding yields a roster of 47.
That roster is a non-exhaustive, protocol-defined discovery cohort, not an enumeration of
the eligible literature; we report its provenance, its disposition flow, and a component
exposure screen over its deposits, none of it as a prevalence estimate.
The target specification these verdicts are conditional on was itself re-derived blind by two
model axes with no access to this project's records; on the fourteen decisions they could
bind, twelve agree that the released evidence does not identify the reported point estimate,
and three cells in our own record were found to name more than one compared quantity.
Reportability and recoverability are independent properties, and separating them requires
matching each evidence operation and claim to what the public record actually supports.
\end{abstract}
